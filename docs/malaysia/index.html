At the desk

The US just proposed new tariffs on Malaysia, so why did the ringgit strengthen against the US dollar yesterday but weaken against the Singapore dollar and the euro?

Against the US dollar the ringgit moved from 4.1170 to 4.1135 at the 6pm close, a 0.09% move, which is small; Bank Muamalat's economist put it down mainly to technical factors. The bigger overnight force was the US dollar itself: the US 10-year yield slipped to 4.87% from 4.91% and the dollar index eased about 0.10%. The Singapore dollar and euro gained more against the US dollar than the ringgit did, so in ringgit terms SGD rose to 3.1878 from 3.1858 and EUR to 4.5133 from 4.5036; a cross rate is just two US dollar rates divided. On tariffs, the US proposal is up to 18% with 25% possible, MITI calls the rate indicative, and public hearings only start on 25 January 2027, so nothing is final yet.

Cross rates: how the ringgit can rise and fall on the same day

Every ringgit cross is built from two US dollar rates. On Thursday USD/MYR fell to 4.1135 from 4.1170, so the ringgit gained 0.09% on the dollar. SGD/MYR rose to 3.1878 from 3.1858, which means USD/SGD (4.1135 / 3.1878) fell to about 1.2904 from 1.2923, so the Singapore dollar gained about 0.15% on the US dollar, more than the ringgit did. The same arithmetic gives EUR/USD of about 1.0972 (4.5133 / 4.1135), up from about 1.0939. So 'the ringgit strengthened' is only true against the dollar; a customer holding SGD or EUR saw those balances worth more in ringgit.

Test yourself: If USD/MYR is 4.1135 and EUR/MYR is 4.5133, what EUR/USD rate do they imply, and which way did the euro move against the dollar if EUR/MYR rose while USD/MYR fell?

Answer (think first)

EUR/USD = 4.5133 / 4.1135, about 1.0972. With EUR/MYR up and USD/MYR down, the euro must have strengthened against the US dollar.
