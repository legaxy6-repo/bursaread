Worth reading

Wall Street erased most of a deep AI-led sell-off on Wednesday, then shut for Thanksgiving, with a hot inflation print locking in bets on a December Fed hike.

  • The S&P 500 fell as much as 3.2% and the Nasdaq as much as 4.6% on Wednesday before recovering; the S&P closed down 0.25% at 7,182.78, the Nasdaq down 0.62% at 23,585.80, and the Dow up 0.33% at 50,182.95. CNBC said no specific catalyst for the bounce could be pinpointed, though traders cited light pre-holiday trading and a Michael Burry blog post.

  • Tech fell as the lift from Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO faded and failed to calm worries about whether the AI trade can last.

  • October PCE inflation rose to 3.8% a year from 3.7%, and core PCE was 3.3%, slightly above the 3.2% forecast. Yet the 10-year Treasury yield fell more than 3bp to about 4.87% as investors bought bonds for protection.

    ReportedCNBC ↗

  • Hotter core inflation, an upward revision to Q3 GDP and a Fed survey showing sticky inflation cemented expectations of a 25bp Fed hike in December and another in early 2027.
